0DIN Defense PRD
PRD: 0DIN Guard Rails
Author: Joe McBride
Status: In Review - Legal Approval Requested
Overview
0DIN is launching a guardrail product line: fast, self-hostable classifier models that sit in front of customer LLM applications and block jailbreaks and prompt injection in real time. The line ships in two forms - an open source community model (SusFactor, trained on older data) to drive adoption, and a commercial model (SusFactor, continuously trained on the latest 0DIN bug bounty data) sold with quality data and support as part of the full line. This PRD defines the product and the first commercial goal, and asks Legal to review the POC motion, open source licensing, training-data rights, and deployment data handling.
Goal
Land 50 proof-of-concept deployments (POCs) with potential buyers within two months - by the close of Black Hat / DEF CON week (early-to-mid August 2026). Leads are sourced from web channels (open source downloads, Product Hunt, Hacker News, and 0din.ai/assess) and from conference channels (Black Hat AI Zone, BSides Las Vegas, DEF CON AI Village). A POC is defined as the model deployed in a prospect's environment, evaluating their traffic, with a named buyer-side owner.
Problem
Teams putting LLM features into production have no guardrail they can both trust and run themselves. Existing options are cloud-only, slow, opaque, or untested against current attacks. 0DIN holds the bug bounty corpus to train the strongest jailbreak classifier available and to prove it with reproducible data.
Product
SusFactor (Open Source / Community)[a][b]
   * Downloadable via Hugging Face tooling.
   * Trained on older 0DIN data.
   * Positioned as the best jailbreak guardrail, backed by reproducible benchmark data.
   * Fast enough to run inline in real-time applications.
   * Small enough to run in CPU-only environments.
SusFactor (Commercial)[c][d]
   * Downloadable license; continuously trained on the most recent 0DIN data.
   * The reach of an open source model with better quality, data, and support.
Supported Implementations
   * LiteLLM proxy - validated with at least one bank; open question is whether the model is fast enough inline in a proxy.
   * Splunk plugin.
   * any-guardrail integration.
   * OpenClaw open source implementation.
Go-to-Market
   * Blog announcement for each release above.
   * Launch posts on Hacker News and Product Hunt.
Out of Scope (Future Releases)
Reporting, attack-intel enrichment, an API-hosted (managed) offering, a Claude Code instance, and a qualitative improvement process. These are noted for roadmap context and are not part of this launch.

Legal Review
The items below are flagged for Legal. References are to the 0DIN Master Services Agreement (MSA, last updated April 2, 2026) and the 0DIN Privacy Notice at 0din.ai/privacy (last updated February 10, 2026).
   1. POCs as Evaluation Services. We intend to run all 50 POCs as Evaluation Services under MSA Section 3 (limited, trial, pilot, or design-partner basis, provided AS IS, no warranties, liability capped at $1,000). Please confirm a lightweight click-through or short-form design-partner SOW is sufficient to place a POC under the MSA, and whether a separate evaluation addendum is needed before a prospect deploys.
   1.    2. Open source license for SusFactor. Recommendation: Apache 2.0, consistent with the Scanner open source release precedent and our Garak dependency. Two items need a decision: (1) the license for the model weights and Hugging Face model card, which may warrant a model-specific license (e.g., OpenRAIL) rather than a code license, and (2) whether a Contributor License Agreement (standard Mozilla CLA or project-specific) is required if we accept community contributions.
   3. Training-data rights. SusFactor is trained on 0DIN bug bounty submissions. Please confirm the Bug Bounty Terms and Conditions and Privacy Notice grant Mozilla sufficient rights to use submissions to train and to distribute a derived model, that training data is free of personal information, and that the reproducible benchmark data we plan to publish is cleared for public release.
   4. Deployment data handling. The guardrail inspects end-user prompts in real time. Default deployment is on-prem and self-hosted, so customer prompt data stays in the customer environment (consistent with MSA Section 5.5: SaaS Services are not HIPAA or PCI compliant, and for on-prem the customer determines what data to use). A future API-hosted version would route prompts through 0DIN infrastructure (GCP and any model subprocessors), which would require Privacy Notice and subprocessor-disclosure updates before launch.
   5. Marketing claims. Best jailbreak guardrail and fast enough for real time are comparative performance claims. We will keep them substantiated with reproducible, published benchmarks; MSA Section 9.2 disclaims warranties, and these should remain marketing claims rather than contractual warranties. Please review the claim language.
   6. Third-party integrations. LiteLLM, Splunk, and OpenClaw are Non-Mozilla Offerings (NMOs) under MSA Section 4. Integration documentation should disclaim NMO support and confirm permitted use of third-party trademarks.
   7. Publicity and naming. The proposed Mozilla AI Security product rename, and any use of POC customers' names or logos, require consent per MSA Section 13.6.
